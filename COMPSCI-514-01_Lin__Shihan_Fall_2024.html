Duke University - Course Evaluations

Fall 2024

Course:

COMPSCI-514-01 : ADVANCED COMPUTER NETWORKS.COMPSCI-514-01.ECE-558-01.

Instructor:

Shihan Lin

1 - What knowledge, methods, skills, insights, or ways of thinking did you develop in this course? Please describe three specific things you learned.

Write-In Responses:
  • Congestion control/routing/data centers
  • Congestion Control, BGP, Software-defined network
  • some key definition such as TCP
  • DNS, BGP, TCP
  • I learned about the foundational goals when building the internet.

    I learned about Valley-Free protocols.

    I learned to test my theories in a test environment representative of a real world scenario.
  • The ability to learn from paper. Self-learning. Cooperation.
  • - I learned about various research areas in computer networks including internet design philosophies, congestion control and principles, routing, BGP, software-defined networking, data center networks, ML for networks/networks for ML, networks security. - I took this class as an undergrad sophomore as my first networks course and read about 22 research papers by the end of this course. I don't fear dense research papers anymore. They can be harder to read but I enjoy picking them up if they're interesting and I think I have a much better understanding of how to approach reading a paper now and how in-depth I should be reading one depending on my goals. - Through my project, I learned how to use ns-3 to conduct networking simulations.
  • reading papers before class
  • I learned a lot in the lectures, including CCAs, routing protocols, data center networks, security and so on. For example, when studying data center networks, I learned about some scalable architectures such as Fat Tree, as well as optical communication-based designs like RotorNet and Opera.
  • I learned how to read research papers and took away important knowledge. I investigated more about cookies and was able to see how invasive tracking technology is. I learned about the foundational knowledge of networking.

2 - Reflecting on the overall learning environment of this class, in what ways did the instructor(s) and the structure or components of the course facilitate your learning? Are there specific course components or methods of instruction you’d keep for future years?

Write-In Responses:
  • The focus on reading and discussing academic papers was useful and made for a great learning environment.
  • The slides help me learn.
  • none
  • I enjoy the reading before classes.
  • The instructor brought in guest lecturers to discuss papers they had recently published after the instructor required us to have read the paper. This combination helped me to not only retain the information in these papers, but to better understand the minute details I missed when reading it.
  • Reading tasks before class is helpful for us to pre-learn the basic content of the class.
  • - The course instructors were very accessible. I went to Professor Yang's office hours several times and she is a great person to talk to. Shihan was also very accessible. The class size was small which I really liked. - Even though this was my first networks course (I was an undergrad sophomore), I didn't really feel lost at any point during the course. Paper readings were due the night before the lecture and then the lecture also covered any background material on the subject that would be needed to motivate/understand the paper. I think the lectures are really good and if you're motivated this class is definitely accessible to undergrads who haven't taken a networks course before.
  • Read relevant papers before class, take notes, answer relevant questions
  • The course is structured in a progressive manner, with both theoretical and experimental components. It includes readings from classic literature as well as cutting-edge papers from top journals. I think that using these readings to introduce the topic of computer networks is a great way to learn—it’s more engaging, rather than explaining each concept in great detail one by one.
  • NA

7 - What would you like to say about this course to a student who is considering taking it in the future?

Write-In Responses:
  • The class definitely requires time and effort. There’s a lot of self learning involved. Some topics are pretty heavy and hard to internalize, but prof yang is so approachable, knowledgeable and helpful.
  • You can really learn something from this course.
  • none
  • Designed for students who want to know more about network
  • Professor Yang is a fantastic teacher and she will do her best to help you learn the concepts. Don't be afraid to reach out to her, especially after class.
  • It is worth learning if you are interested in network knowledge.
  • - Take this course with Prof. Yang!
  • This course is very interesting, and the workload is quite manageable. Even if you don’t have a strong foundation in computer networks, as long as you understand the basics, such as the fundamental principles of common protocols like TCP and BGP, you can follow the course content. However, it’s important to prepare in advance; otherwise, it can be hard to keep up with the instructor’s pace during class.

    Here’s a tip: for newer topics, some of the papers might be conference papers, and you can often find the authors’ presentation videos, which are usually around 15 minutes long. These can serve as excellent preparatory material.
